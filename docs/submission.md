# Digitizing the IUPAC Solubility Data Series

Solubility decides the outcome of many applications in chemistry and chemical engineering, and much of the data behind them was acquired and published long before the emergence of digital technology. One of the largest curated collection of these laboratory reports is the IUPAC Solubility Data Series: 56 scanned volumes, about 21 000 pages of fixed-form data sheets, dated back as early as 1855 up to the year 1990. Only eight of the 56 volumes were ever keyed by hand into the IUPAC-NIST Solubility Database. Vision-language models (VLMs) read modern layouts reliably but struggle with typewritten and hand-set pages, and it is an open question how to score an extraction for both completeness and accuracy. Our pipeline, **SolTractor**, addresses both, using the  IUPAC-NIST Solubility Database as ground truth for performance assessment. The pipeline is structured as follows:

- **Two complementary readings per page.** The embedded ClearScan text layer carries 97 % of the hand-keyed values verbatim (volume 60) and their exact positions on the page. The document VLM MinerU 2.5-Pro adds the sheet structure, returning every sheet as a table of its printed boxes and finding sheets on pages with faint headers (success rate of 30 out of 32 in volume 58).
- **Row structure from crops.** On a whole page, the VLM treats the experimental-values table as a single box and runs its lines together. We therefore cut this band from every sheet, located through the text layer's word positions, so the VLM reads it as a table of its own: on the eight ground-truth volumes, two thirds of the 17 190 hand-keyed rows come back intact.
- **Accuracy without ground truth.** Compilers printed most quantities twice, linked by closed formulas. On the eight ground-truth volumes, 98 % of more than 21 000 recomputed values agree within the last printed digit; the checks pinpoint misread powers of ten and shifted columns.
- **FAIR export, LLMs for language.** Every table row becomes one equilibrium-solution entry in a harmonized schema on NOMAD, an open research-data platform, with provenance down to page and row: 12 786 validated entries from the ground-truth volumes, 88 % with at least one solubility quantity, not yet public. A fixed lookup assigns seven in eight columns their quantity and unit; an LLM interprets the rest, and the LLM Qwen3.8 maps the IUPAC-NIST records into the same schema. Every number an LLM returns must occur verbatim in its source. The pipeline now runs on the 46 volumes without ground truth.

Rejuvenating the series according to the FAIR principles turns decades of curated measurements into a machine-readable resource for machine-learning models of solubility, which we showcase by predicting solubilities on held-out test sets.
